Tales of Velmora

Five places on the grounds remember everyone who walks through them, and some of them will answer if you say the right thing. These pages were left behind by the students who found out.

How to read these pages

Every page is written in the past, about something that already happened. Watch for the moment a writer forgets that, and says what they do, as if it is happening right now, or speaks straight to the place itself. Those words are exactly what the place was waiting to hear.

  • Explore the place first with /explore, then type the words in that same channel within the hour.
  • Mind who signed the page. Some places answer only to the writer's own house.
  • Mind the hour. Some words only work after dark, between 8 PM and 6 AM, Velmora (Central) time, and some only in daylight.
  • Some places only give gifts to people they already love. Others only accept an apology from people they have turned against.
  • The Garden has strange weeks. Words written about them only work while it is happening.

Most secrets answer each student only once. A few answer only the very first student to find them.

🌿  The Garden

The Visitors' Book in the Gardener's Shed

Nobody knows who first left a blank book on the potting bench inside the old gardener's shed, or why the shed is always unlocked from the outside and never from within. Students have been writing in it for as long as the Garden has been remembering them. These are the pages that survived the damp.

A Caldrin first-yearEarly morning

Nobody warned me the Garden had manners. I came in under the rose arch half asleep, and it felt exactly like walking into someone's house without knocking. So I stopped on the path, the way my grandmother taught me to stop in a doorway, and I said it out loud.

Good morning, roses.

They turned to look at me. Every bloom on the Rose Walk, one after another, like a row of heads at a lecture. I kept walking and they kept watching. It was unnerving. It was also, I decided later, polite.

A Vashara second-yearLate afternoon

Everyone told me the Ancient Tree was just old. It wasn't just old. I went to sit beneath it after a rotten day and ended up leaning back against the trunk with my eyes shut, and then, for no reason I could name, I turned my head. I press my ear to the Ancient Tree.

Something deep inside it was counting. Slowly. Patiently. It reached a number, stopped, and started again from one. I counted heads at dinner that night. I was fairly sure the numbers matched.

A Moonveil third-yearTwo visits, a week apart

The Reflection Pool whispered whenever nobody was standing near it, and went quiet the moment anyone was. I wanted to know whether it was shy or rude, so I knelt on the mossy steps and gave it something to work with. I whisper my name into the pool. It whispered one back. Not mine. I didn't recognise it.

A week later I went back, because now it knew me, and a thing that knows your name will usually talk. I ask the pool a question. It didn't answer in words. It showed me a corridor in the Library I had never walked down, and at the end of it a cabinet with a very small keyhole. It would not have shown me that on the first visit. Introductions matter.

A Veyren fourth-yearMidday

Down in the Mushroom Hollow there was a perfect ring of pale caps, and everyone knew the rule about rings. I was never good with rules. I step into the mushroom ring.

The world went green and very quiet. When I stepped back out, my pockets were heavier and my shoes were on the wrong feet. The Garden was cooler with me for a while afterwards. It was a fair trade, all things considered.

A Thornmere fifth-yearPast eleven at night

The Moonbloom Meadow in daylight was pretty. After dark it was something else entirely: half the blooms shut tight, half of them trembling, all of them waiting. My friend dared me. I didn't need the dare. I sing to the moonblooms.

Every flower in the meadow opened at once. One of them let go a petal straight into my hand. It wouldn't have happened at noon. The meadow only listens once the sun is gone.

A Caldrin sixth-yearNoon, and later that week

The broken sundial by the path had been wrong for a century, and it offended me personally. At noon, with the sun full on its face, I put both hands on the gnomon. I turn the sundial. For one second every shadow in the Garden jumped an hour, and somewhere a bell rang that should not have rung. The Garden sulked at me for days. But I came away with a cracked crystal that had been wedged beneath the dial, and I kept it.

Later that week I carried it to the statues nobody remembers commissioning. One of them holds out an empty stone palm, as if it has been waiting a long time for somebody to fill it. You cannot do this part empty-handed. I place a crystal in the stone hand. The fingers closed, very slowly. When they opened again, there was something else in them.

A Veyren first-yearAfter lessons

This shed, the one that keeps this very book, has a door that has never once opened for me. I had written in here a dozen times before it occurred to me that I had never asked properly. I knock on the gardener's shed.

Three knocks came back from inside. The door stayed shut. Underneath it, somebody slid out a folded scrap of map.

A Vashara fifth-yearA grey morning

Under the weeping willow by the stream, the hanging branches had grown together into a doorway, neat as if someone had woven it. I had walked past it every week for a year. That morning I didn't walk past. I walk through the willow door.

It led into the same Garden, only quieter and a little older. When I turned around, I was somehow standing in front of the doorway again, and I had something in my pocket that hadn't been there.

A Thornmere second-yearTwo in the morning

They say the barn owl in the owl oak has only ever been waiting to speak once, to the first student polite enough to ask, and that after that it will never say another word to anyone. I went out at two in the morning, because owls keep owl hours. I bow to the owl.

It looked at me for a very long time. Then it looked past me, over my shoulder, as if it were expecting someone else. Whoever gets there first gets the words. It is still waiting.

A Vashara seventh-yearMy last week, after seven years of visits

By my final year the Garden knew my footsteps. Roses leaned in when I passed. The paths ran straight for me when they twisted for everyone else. My friends had dared me since first year to ask it for something, and I never had, because you don't ask a stranger. It had not been a stranger for a long time. I ask the Garden for a gift.

A single white orchid grew up out of the path, opened, and let itself be picked. It would not have done that for anyone it didn't love.

A Moonveil fourth-year, who is not proud of thisLate autumn

I spent most of a term being awful to this place. I trampled the beds for shortcuts, pocketed whatever glittered, and laughed at the roses. By December the thorns closed across every path I tried, and the Pool went silent whenever I came near. I finally understood that it wasn't going to forgive me unless I said it. I apologise to the Garden.

The roses didn't open for me. But the thorns across the path drew back, just a little. It's a start. It only mattered because things had gone so badly. If the Garden already likes you, there's nothing to apologise for.

A Thornmere fourth-yearFull dark

At the edge of the brambles, where the paths run out, I heard wolves. Not near. Not far. Every Thornmere knows what that sound does to your chest. I didn't think about it. I howl at the brambles.

For a long moment, nothing. Then, deep in the brambles, something howled back, and a crown of thorns rolled out onto the path at my feet. It only answers the wolf's own house, and only once it's dark.

A Veyren sixth-yearDusk

There is a white stag at the edge of the Moonbloom Meadow that everyone claims to have seen and nobody can describe. I saw it. I didn't run toward it and I didn't reach for it. Veyren don't grab. I bow to the white stag.

When I looked up, it bowed back, and then it was gone. There was a hare's whisker on the grass where it had stood, silver as frost.

A Vashara third-yearA wet afternoon

Somebody had snapped a flower on the Rose Walk and left it hanging by a thread. Nobody else stopped. Vashara always stop. I heal the broken flower.

I cupped the stem between my hands and felt it straighten. It bloomed right there, and every flower nearby turned to look at me like I had done something important. Maybe I had.

A Caldrin fifth-yearWell after curfew

On a clear night the Reflection Pool doesn't reflect tonight's sky. I noticed because I was charting it, and the pattern was wrong by centuries. I knelt with a pencil behind my ear. I read the stars in the pool.

One of the reflected stars was labelled, in tiny faded handwriting, in a hand I later found only once in the Library. The Pool shows its old sky only after dark, and only to someone who charts it properly.

A Moonveil sixth-yearAfternoon

Controlled conditions are the whole point. One meadow, a hundred moonblooms, one variable. I wrote the method on my wrist. I test a hypothesis on the moonblooms.

I changed the one variable, and every bloom in the meadow changed colour to match. The Garden, it turns out, enjoys a good experiment, as long as the person running it is one of us.

The Strange Weeks

At the back of the book, in a different, older hand, the Gardener kept a list of the weeks when the Garden did something it doesn't usually do. These pages only mean anything while it is happening. When the Garden is quiet, they are just stories.

The GardenerThe week a door appeared behind the roses

One morning there was a door behind the roses that had not been there the night before, set into nothing, leading nowhere. It stayed for a few days, and then it didn't. While it was there, the students who knew their manners did the obvious thing. I knock on the door behind the roses. It never opened. Things slid out underneath it.

The GardenerThe week of the golden frog

A frog made of what looked like solid gold came up out of the stream for a handful of days. It likes a bargain more than it likes anyone. Bring it something and see what it thinks your something is worth. I trade with the golden frog. It will deal as often as you care to, for as long as it stays.

The GardenerThe week a key lay beneath the bench

A cold iron key appeared beneath the crooked bench outside the Reading Alcove. Half the school walked past it assuming it belonged to someone. One student knelt. I take the key. There is only ever one key, and it goes to whoever is quickest.

The GardenerThe week of the unexplored path

A path opened through the hedge that was certainly not there the day before. Most people stared at it. I walk the unexplored path. At its end, each time, something was waiting, as if it had been left there for whoever came.

The GardenerThe week the Pool called a name

Some weeks the Reflection Pool whispers a single student's name, over and over, and nobody else can make it stop. If it is your name, you know what to do. I answer the pool. If it isn't your name, the Pool doesn't want to hear from you.

The GardenerThe week the Ancient Tree glowed

For a few nights the Ancient Tree glowed from the inside, faint and gold, like a lamp behind thick glass. It was warm to stand near. I touch the glowing tree. Those who did said they could hear every root in the Garden at once.

📚  The Library

Notes in the Back of Volume Seven

Volume Seven of the Library's history has never once been checked out, and yet the borrowing card glued inside its back cover is full. Readers have written on it and around it and underneath it for longer than the card has existed. Nobody admits to having started.

A Caldrin second-yearA Monday

I kept this book for six weeks longer than I was allowed, and every day it was overdue the reference desk looked at me a little harder. Nobody sits at that desk. It still looks. On the Monday I couldn't stand it anymore. I return the overdue book.

When I turned back, the book and the fine were both gone, and the desk smelled faintly of dust and relief. Anyone can settle up. You just have to actually do it.

A Moonveil fifth-yearOne in the morning

The Library closes at eight. It doesn't close. It just stops pretending to be open. I hid in the periodicals until the lamps went out and the shelves grew taller in the dark. I read past curfew.

Something on the highest shelf fluttered once, like a page turning by itself, like it was listening back. It only does that after dark. During the day it's just a library.

A Thornmere fourth-yearBetween lessons

The lock on the Restricted Section is old and proud and, in my professional opinion, mostly for show. Thornmere don't wait to be let in. I pick the Restricted Section lock.

It resisted me, then gave all at once. Nothing on the other side noticed me at all, which was somehow worse. I came out with a diary that has a lock of its own.

A Veyren sixth-yearEvening, the reading room nearly empty

Some books don't want to be read so much as kept company. I found one in the long reading room and I didn't open it. I sit in silence with the book.

I didn't turn a page for the better part of an hour. Something settled in across the table from me and did the same. When I left, there was a pressed violet between the covers. Only a Veyren would have had the patience.

A Vashara third-yearA rainy afternoon

Somebody had torn a page nearly out of an old herbal and shoved it back on the shelf. It made my hands ache just looking at it. Vashara mend things. It's not a choice. I mend the torn page.

I smoothed it flat between my palms, and when I looked again the tear was gone and the ink had healed shut behind it. There was a nib on the table I hadn't brought. It writes by itself.

A Caldrin fifth-yearA long Saturday

The card catalogue has been wrong since before anyone alive was born, and nobody trusts it, and nobody fixes it. I brought a system. Caldrin always bring a system. I recatalogue the shelf.

I worked through the drawer by method, not guesswork. The last card I filed was for a book that hasn't been written yet. I kept the bookmark it was clipped to.

A Moonveil fourth-yearNo idea. I lost track.

Page 214 had a footnote. The footnote cited a book. That book had a footnote. You see where this is going, and so did I, and I went anyway, because I was Moonveil through and through, and that was the whole point of us. I follow the footnote.

I lost twenty minutes and gained an idea I still can't put down. There was a scrap of someone else's margin notes at the end of the trail. They'd been following it too.

A Veyren seventh-yearMy final term, after years of visits

I had been coming there since my first week. The Library knew the sound of my chair on the floor. Books fell open to the right page for me. Only after all that did I feel I had any right to ask it for anything. I ask the Library for a gift.

A single volume slid itself an inch off a high shelf, waiting for me to notice. Nobody else could see it move. It would not have moved for a stranger.

A Thornmere third-year, reluctantlyWinter

I dog-eared the first editions, talked through every silence, and left a sandwich in the map room. The Library started losing my books on purpose and slamming drawers when I reached for them. When a place turns on you that hard, there's only one way back. I apologise to the Library.

I said it to the empty reading room. A drawer that had been stuck for weeks slid open on its own, just a crack. If it had never turned on me, there would have been nothing to say.

🕯️  The Dungeons

The Old Detention Log

The Dungeons were used for detention long after they stopped being used for anything worse, and whoever served it had to sign the log. The log was never collected. Students kept writing in it after detentions ended, which says something about the Dungeons, or about students.

A Veyren first-yearDetention, hour two

They gave me three hours and nothing to do. The cells line the corridor with their doors rusted open, and there was nothing to look at but them, so eventually I did the only thing left to do. I count the old cells.

I counted twelve. I counted again and got eleven. I decided not to count a third time. There was a pinch of grey dust on my sleeve afterwards that wouldn't brush off.

A Caldrin fourth-yearNearly midnight

There's a drainage grate halfway down the long corridor that hums faintly during the day. At night it doesn't hum. It does something else. I listen at the drainage grate at night.

Something below the grate was breathing in a rhythm that wasn't mine. I backed away slowly and didn't look down again. In daylight it only hums. Don't bother before dark.

A Thornmere third-yearDetention, hour one

The old manacles still hang from the wall of the end cell, and everyone says they'll hold you if you touch them. Thornmere don't take "everyone says" as an answer. I test the manacles.

The chain held, and then it didn't. It was never locked. It was only old enough to look like it was. I pocketed a link. Nobody down there objected.

A Veyren fifth-yearEvening

There is a small shrine cut into the wall down here, for someone nobody remembers. People walk past it talking. I didn't want to walk past it. I stand silent at the shrine.

I stood with it a long while and said nothing. Nothing answered. But the cold in that part of the corridor eased, a little, just for me.

A Vashara fourth-yearLate afternoon

The same shrine. The last candle on it was a stub, and there was dust on everything, and it made me sad in a way I couldn't explain. Vashara don't leave a thing like that uncared for. I light a candle at the shrine.

It caught, though I hadn't brought a match. Whoever that shrine was for, they were grateful. I could feel it. There was ash left on my fingers that smelled like summer.

A Caldrin sixth-yearA free period

The iron door at the end of the corridor is sealed and cold, and everyone assumes it's locked. Assumptions are unmeasured data. I study the iron door's lock.

I traced the mechanism with a fingertip until it made sense. It isn't locked at all. It was simply never meant to open outward. I kept the chalk I used to sketch it; the marks it makes don't show in lamplight.

A Moonveil third-yearDetention, hour three (voluntary)

The disused alchemy room still has residue in half its beakers, and nobody has labelled anything in a century. I took that as an invitation. I run an experiment in the alchemy room.

I changed one variable. The residue in the beaker changed colour to match my house scarf. I did not expect that. It will only play along with its own kind of curiosity.

A Thornmere seventh-yearAfter years of coming down here on purpose

Nobody comes to the Dungeons because they like them. I did, eventually. By my last year the torches lit themselves a step before I reached them, and the drip in the cistern slowed when I passed. Only then did I dare. I ask the dungeons for a gift.

Something rolled out from under the storeroom door and stopped, deliberately, at my feet: an old coin nobody spends anymore. The Dungeons don't give strangers anything.

A Vashara second-year, embarrassinglySpring

I carved my initials in a cell wall, kicked a bucket down the stairwell to hear it echo, and laughed at the shrine. After that, every chain in the long corridor dragged whenever I came near, and the torches went out one by one ahead of me. It only stopped when I said it. I apologise to the dungeons.

I said it into the dark. Somewhere, a chain that had been dragging stopped dragging. It only counts if the Dungeons have already turned against you.

🌲  The Forbidden Woods

Field Notes Found in the Hunting Blind

An oilcloth notebook was found wedged in the rafters of the half-collapsed hunting blind at the edge of the Woods. Its pages were written by many hands, most of them in a hurry, all of them somewhere they had been told not to be.

A Caldrin third-yearAfternoon

Among the standing stones there's a flat one, flatter than the others, and it's always clean, as if someone tends it. I had a ribbon in my pocket and no better use for it. I leave an offering at the old shrine stones.

I set it on the flattest stone and walked away. When I glanced back from the treeline, it was already gone. There was a blackened acorn on the path in front of me that hadn't been there a moment before.

A Veyren fourth-yearWell past dark

At night something howls along the wolf trail, far off and patient. Everyone freezes when they hear it. I stopped freezing. I answer the howling.

Something far away answered me, closer the second time. A single black feather drifted down out of nowhere and landed on my sleeve. The trail is only that loud once the sun goes down.

A Thornmere fifth-yearDawn

The tracks along the old trail are too large to be a fox's and too careful to be a dog's. Most people turn around where they fade. Thornmere don't. I follow the wolf trail.

I followed the prints past where they should have stopped. Whatever made them was long gone, but it had left something behind on purpose: a shed fang, still sharp.

A Veyren sixth-yearGrey morning

The ring of standing stones has been there longer than the castle. People climb on them and carve their names in them. Veyren don't. I bow to the standing stones.

I bowed low and quiet. Nothing moved. But I would have sworn, walking away, that the stones were standing a little straighter than before, and a chip of one of them was lying at my feet.

A Vashara fourth-yearA bitter afternoon

The stream in the Woods is frozen in every season, even in June, and something downstream always sounds thirsty. It hurt to hear. I clear the frozen stream.

I worked the ice loose with bare, aching hands until the water moved underneath. Something downstream breathed easier for it. There was a little charm of woven root on the bank when I stood up.

A Caldrin fifth-yearAll day

The deep thicket has swallowed three of my friends' afternoons, and nobody has ever written down a way through it. That struck me as a failure of record-keeping. I map a path through the thicket.

I marked the trees as I went so I wouldn't lose the way back. By the end I had charted a route nobody had bothered to write down. The Woods seemed to respect the effort.

A Moonveil fourth-yearDusk, for science

Someone left a bottle of witchlight oil in the hollow log, which is either a trap or a gift, and there's only one way to know which. I test the witchlight.

I dabbed a little on a dead leaf, just to see. It didn't burn. It glowed, cold and blue, for far longer than it should have. I kept the bottle. It was clearly meant for someone curious enough.

A Moonveil seventh-yearAfter years of trespassing

I came out here so often that the brambles stopped catching my robes. The mist parted for me in the clearing. The Woods had decided, somewhere along the way, that I belonged to them. Only then did I ask. I ask the woods for a gift.

Something pale and quiet stepped out of the treeline just long enough to leave a gift at my feet, then was gone before I could look up. The Woods don't give anything to someone they barely know.

A Caldrin second-year, who should have known betterAutumn

I broke branches for no reason, threw stones into the black pond, and shouted just to hear the echo. After that the paths closed in behind me and in front of me, and I got lost within sight of the treeline. There was only one thing left to try. I apologise to the woods.

I said it out loud to the trees. The path ahead, which had been closing in, opened back up by a few feet. The Woods only want an apology from someone who has earned one.

🔭  The Observatory

The Night Log of the Tower

Every telescope in the Observatory comes with a log, and every log is supposed to hold coordinates. This one drifted. Somewhere around its third page, the observers stopped recording the sky and started recording what the tower did back.

A Veyren second-yearAfternoon

There's a brass compass in the lens-grinding workshop that has never once pointed north. Everyone calls it broken. I thought it might just have somewhere else in mind. I follow the broken compass.

It didn't point anywhere sensible. I followed it anyway, up the spiral stair, into a corner I had never noticed, and found a fragment of a star chart that didn't match any sky I knew.

A Vashara fifth-yearPast midnight

On the main viewing deck, well after dark, I found six stars nobody had ever joined together. They looked lonely. I name a new constellation.

I traced the shape between them and gave it a name. For the rest of that night, at least, it was mine. In daylight there's nothing up there to name.

A Thornmere sixth-yearAll night, until sunrise

The comet-watching balcony is freezing, exposed, and famous for making people give up and go to bed. That was all the invitation I needed. I stay out on the balcony until dawn.

My fingers went numb somewhere around the third hour. I stayed anyway. When dawn came, it came for me specifically, and it brought a sliver of something that fell out of the sky.

A Veyren fifth-yearEvening

Everyone on the main deck talks: about the stars, about each other, about the cold. I didn't want to add to it. I watch the sky in silence.

I didn't move for a long while. Somewhere above me, a star seemed to hold as still as I did. When I finally looked down, there was a chart fragment tucked under my boot.

A Vashara fourth-yearA slow afternoon

The orrery in the corner has been broken for years. One arm bent, one planet stuck, the whole thing sagging like it's tired. It hurt to look at. I repair the broken orrery.

I eased the bent arm back into its track, gently, until it clicked. The whole model shuddered, then started turning again for the first time in years. It gave me one of its spare gears, probably as thanks.

A Caldrin sixth-yearLate, clear sky

The atlases on the dusty shelf are three hundred years old and still mostly right. Mostly. I found a point of light that wasn't on any of them, and I was never the kind of person who left a gap in a record. I chart a new star.

I marked its position carefully and checked the old charts twice. It isn't on either of them. I was the first to write it down, and the page I wrote it on still glitters.

A Moonveil fifth-yearWhenever. Time is a construct.

The oldest telescope in the tower has a lens that isn't glass, exactly. Nobody has ever swapped it out to see what the telescope does without it, which is frankly irresponsible. I test the telescope's lens.

I swapped the lens for a moment, just to see. What came into focus wasn't the sky at all, and I only got a glimpse before it was gone. I kept the lens. For further study.

A Caldrin seventh-yearAfter years of nights up here

I had spent more nights in that tower than in my own bed. By then the dome opened before I reached for the lever, and the telescopes swung toward whatever I was about to look for. It took years before I felt I could ask it for anything. I ask the Observatory for a gift.

A vial that wasn't on the shelf a moment earlier was on the shelf, warm to the touch and faintly glowing. The tower keeps its gifts for the people it knows.

A Moonveil third-year, sheepishlyWinter

I scratched a lens, spilled ink across a star chart, and left the dome open in a snowstorm. After that the dome refused to open for me at all, and every telescope I touched showed only cloud. There was one thing left to try. I apologise to the Observatory.

I said it up into the dark of the dome, and the dome, slowly, creaked back open on its own. It only works if the tower has actually turned against you.
